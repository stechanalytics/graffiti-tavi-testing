Introduction
 
Minimum Spanning Technologies Ltd. (MSTL) was founded on 28 June 2017 as a private limited company based in London, United Kingdom. In 2018, management relocated to Berlin.

Following the United Kingdom’s withdrawal from the European Union, MSTL has been treated as a partnership (OHG) under German civil law since 1 January 2021. MSTL is the predecessor of the German-registered MST OHG (Minimum Spanning Technologies OHG), which was formally entered in the Berlin commercial register on 28 September 2021. On 29 November 2024, the company was renamed to Minimum Spanning Technologies UG (haftungsbeschränkt) & Co. OHG. Operational activities and ownership structure remain unchanged.

The business purpose of MSTL is the development of a blockchain-based network whose members provide and exchange virtual storage capacities. The so-called "AR Token", which was also developed by MSTOHG/MSTL and is sold by MSTOHG/MSTL, serves to utilize this network. The AR token is used as a "means of payment" within the AR network and can therefore also serve as an investment object. The proceeds from the sale of AR tokens are partly reinvested by MSTOHG/MSTL in companies that develop products and services based on the AR network. MSTOHG/MSTL operates globally with customers and business partners worldwide.

To fulfill its corporate purpose, MSTOHG maintains a service agreement with Cluster 227 UG. C-227 provides software development services and business operations services.

Business review
 
In 2023, the Arweave network continued to scale, with total network storage increasing from 114.05 TiB to 147 TiB — a 29% year-over-year increase. The total number of transactions on the network rose from 459.9 million to over 2.2 billion.

MSTL generated total revenue of €27,236,401 (prior year: €39,032,616), primarily from the monetisation of AR tokens.
 
Additionally, MSTL expanded its lending operations. It realised €4,073,277 in interest income (prior year: € 1,154,504) from loan agreements with affiliated entities and other investments.

The Company is financed primarily through proceeds from prior AR token sales. In line with management’s strategic decision, significantly fewer tokens were sold in 2023 compared to 2021 and 2022. Liquidity remained stable throughout the year, with sufficient cash and token reserves to meet all obligations.

The Company has no outstanding debt and has not raised external equity. Its financial position remains solid, supported by disciplined cash management and conservative spending.

In 2024 and 2025, the Company chose not to fund operations through large-scale token sales, instead relying on existing liquidity and selectively expanding its investment portfolio. The strategic focus remained on strengthening the AR ecosystem through strategic investments and support.

Operating costs were relatively stable. Depreciation charges on token-related financial assets decreased significantly to €921,321.78 (prior year: €7,496,946.44), reflecting a partial market recovery.

Principal risks and uncertainties
 
Key risks identified by management include:

 • Cybersecurity risk: MSTL operates with robust internal controls to mitigate the risk of unauthorised access or  cyberattacks on its IT systems and crypto-assets.
 • Loss of crypto-assets: Secure custody arrangements and internal processes are designed to prevent the loss or mismanagement of token holdings.
 • Investment impairment risk: Most investments are made in early-stage companies with direct relationships to MSTL. Close involvement from MSTL experts in product and strategy serves to safeguard investment value.

Despite ongoing risks, MSTL continues to benefit from the structural adoption of decentralised storage and increasing transaction volumes on the Arweave network.

Financial key performance indicators
 
The directors consider the following financial metrics to be key indicators of performance for the Company:

• Revenue for the year amounted to €27.24 million (2022: €39.03 million), primarily arising from sales of AR tokens. The significant decrease reflects management’s deliberate decision to minimise AR token sales in 2023.
• Interest income from lending activities to group companies increased to €4.07 million (2022: €1.15 million), driven by higher outstanding loan balances and increased utilisation of credit lines by affiliated entities.
• The cost of materials, which primarily relates to consulting services and freelancers, decreased in 2023 and amounted to €3.74 million (prior year: €8.50 million).
• Depreciation and amortisation on token-related financial assets decreased significantly to €0.92 million (2022: €7.50 million), in line with improved token price stability and fewer impairments.

Other key performance indicators
 
In addition to financial results, the directors monitor operational and ecosystem development metrics:

• The Arweave network grew substantially, with stored data increasing from 114.05 TiB to 147.00 TiB, a 29% year-over-year increase.
• Transaction activity on the network surged, with total transactions exceeding 2.2 billion by year-end (2022: 459.9 million), reflecting strong adoption of the underlying infrastructure.
• The Company maintained its role as a key contributor to the ecosystem, providing technical, financial, and strategic resources to a broad network of builders and developers.

Opinion


We have audited the financial statements of Minimum Spanning Technologies Limited (the 'Company') for the year ended 31 December 2023, which comprise the Statement of Comprehensive Income, the Statement of Financial Position, the Statement of Cash Flows, the Statement of Changes in Equity and the related notes, including a summary of significant accounting policies. The financial reporting framework that has been applied in their preparation is applicable law and United Kingdom Accounting Standards, including Financial Reporting Standard 102 ‘The Financial Reporting Standard applicable in the UK and Republic of Ireland' (United Kingdom Generally Accepted Accounting Practice).


In our  opinion, except for the possible effects of the matter described in the Basis for Qualified Opinion section of our report,  the financial statements:


∙give a true and fair view of the state of the Company's affairs as at 31 December 2023 and of its profit for the year then ended;
∙have been properly prepared in accordance with United Kingdom Generally Accepted Accounting Practice; and
∙have been prepared in accordance with the requirements of the Companies Act 2006.


Basis for Qualified Opinion


We were appointed as auditors of the Company subsequent to the prior financial year and were therefore unable to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence regarding the AR sales deferred revenue of €128,374,792 as at 31 December 2023 and €145,484,956 as at 31 December 2022 and AR token cost basis €4,947,786 as at 31 December 2023 and €1,331,825 as at 31 December 2022, which forms part of inventories, as at the beginning of the current reporting period.

The AR sales deferred revenue balance relates to AR token sales that occurred in the prior year. As the opening balance of deferred revenue affects the timing and amount of revenue recognised in the current and subsequent financial years, we were unable to determine whether any adjustments might be necessary in respect of revenue, profit for the year, deferred revenue at the reporting date, or the related disclosures. 

The AR token cost basis relates to AR tokens held by the Company and recorded within inventories at cost. As we were unable to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence in respect of this opening balance, we were unable to determine whether any adjustments might be necessary to inventories, cost of sales, profit for the year, or the related disclosures in the current and subsequent financial years.


We conducted our  audit in accordance with International Standards on Auditing (UK) (ISAs (UK)) and applicable law. Our responsibilities under those standards are further described in the Auditors' responsibilities for the audit of the financial statements section of our  report. We are independent of the Company in accordance with the ethical requirements that are relevant to our  audit of the financial statements in the United Kingdom, including the Financial Reporting Council's Ethical Standard and we have fulfilled our  other ethical responsibilities in accordance with these requirements. We believe that the audit evidence we have obtained is sufficient and appropriate to provide a basis for our  opinion.

2.3

Foreign currency translation

Functional and presentation currency

The Company's functional and presentational currency is Euros.

Transactions and balances

Foreign currency transactions are translated into the functional currency using the spot exchange rates at the dates of the transactions.

At each period end foreign currency monetary items are translated using the closing rate. Non-monetary items measured at historical cost are translated using the exchange rate at the date of the transaction and non-monetary items measured at fair value are measured using the exchange rate when fair value was determined.

Foreign exchange gains and losses resulting from the settlement of transactions and from the translation at period-end exchange rates of monetary assets and liabilities denominated in foreign currencies are recognised in profit or loss except when deferred in other comprehensive income as qualifying cash flow hedges.

Foreign exchange gains and losses that relate to borrowings and cash and cash equivalents are presented in the Statement of Comprehensive Income within 'finance income or costs'. All other foreign exchange gains and losses are presented in profit or loss within 'other operating income'.

  
2.4

Revenue

Revenue is recognised to the extent that it is probable that the economic benefits will flow to the Company and the revenue can be reliably measured. Revenue is measured as the fair value of the consideration received or receivable, excluding discounts, rebates, value added tax and other sales taxes.

Revenue comprises consideration received in exchange for digital assets. Revenue is recognised over the period which the holder of the digital asset would receive benefit from the Company's performance. Revenue is deferred in excess of costs until the point at which the platform ceases to operate, or the Company is no longer engaged in the development and activities to support the platform, at which point the Company will have satisfied its obligation to the owner of the digital asset.

Where uncertainty arises over the outcome of the transaction, revenue shall not be deferred in excess of 10 years.

Interest income is recognised in profit or loss using the effective interest method.

2.12

Financial instruments

The Company only enters into basic financial instrument transactions that result in the recognition of financial assets and liabilities like trade and other debtors and creditors, loans from banks and other third parties, loans to related parties and investments in ordinary shares.

Debt instruments (other than those wholly repayable or receivable within one year), including loans and other accounts receivable and payable, are initially measured at present value of the future cash flows and subsequently at amortised cost using the effective interest method. Debt instruments that are payable or receivable within one year, typically trade debtors and creditors, are measured, initially and subsequently, at the undiscounted amount of the cash or other consideration expected to be paid or received. However, if the arrangements of a short-term instrument constitute a financing transaction, like the payment of a trade debt deferred beyond normal business terms or in case of an out-right short-term loan that is not at market rate, the financial asset or liability is measured, initially at the present value of future cash flows discounted at a market rate of interest for a similar debt instrument and subsequently at amortised cost, unless it qualifies as a loan from a director in the case of a small company, or a public benefit entity concessionary loan.

Investments in non-derivative instruments that are equity to the issuer are measured:

•  at fair value with changes recognised in the Statement of Comprehensive Income if the shares are publicly traded or their fair value can otherwise be measured reliably;
•  at cost less impairment for all other investments.

Financial assets that are measured at cost and amortised cost are assessed at the end of each
reporting period for objective evidence of impairment. If objective evidence of impairment is found,
an impairment loss is recognised in the Statement of Comprehensive Income.

For financial assets measured at amortised cost, the impairment loss is measured as the difference between an asset's carrying amount and the present value of estimated cash flows discounted at the asset's original effective interest rate. If a financial asset has a variable interest rate, the discount rate for measuring any impairment loss is the current effective interest rate determined under the contract.

For financial assets measured at cost less impairment, the impairment loss is measured as the difference between an asset's carrying amount and best estimate of the recoverable amount, which is an approximation of the amount that the Company would receive for the asset if it were to be sold at the reporting date.

Financial assets and liabilities are offset and the net amount reported in the Statement of Financial Position when there is an enforceable right to set off the recognised amounts and there is an intention to settle on a net basis or to realise the asset and settle the liability simultaneously.

3.


Judgments in applying accounting policies and key sources of estimation uncertainty

In the application of the Company's accounting policies, the director is required to make judgements, estimates and assumptions about the carrying amounts of assets and liabilities that are not readily apparent from other sources. The estimates and associated assumptions are based on historical experience and other factors that are considered to be relevant. Actual results may differ from these estimates.

The estimates and underlying assumptions are reviewed on an ongoing basis. Revisions to accounting estimates are recognised in the period in which the estimate is revised where the revision affects only that period, or in the period of the revision and future periods where the revision affects both current and future periods.

Corporation Tax and VAT Provision
The entity has recognised a provision for German corporation tax and German VAT of €58,636,447  and €1,722,994, respectively. The provision is based on estimates and assumptions on undeclared policies which the entity is expecting to be applicable to their trade.

Deferred Revenue
The Company recognises deferred revenue in respect of amounts received in advance for services to be provided over the expected period of network development and operation. Management has exercised significant judgement in determining the period over which this deferred revenue is recognised as income.
 
The deferred revenue is amortised over a period of ten years, which represents management’s best estimate of the period over which the Company will continue to provide substantive services prior to the network becoming sufficiently decentralised such that the Company’s ongoing involvement is expected to reduce significantly.

This estimate is based on current development plans, anticipated technological milestones, and industry experience. Actual outcomes may differ from these estimates, and any changes to the expected period of decentralisation could result in a material adjustment to the timing of revenue recognised in future periods.

Valuation impairment estimates 
The Company holds investments in SAFTs, SAFEs, TPAs and convertible notes, which are classified as financial assets and are measured at fair value at each reporting date where observable market data is available. Determining fair value in these circumstances requires the use of judgement, particularly where market activity is limited or prices are subject to volatility.

Where reliable fair value information is not available, such investments are measured at cost less impairment. In assessing whether an impairment has occurred, management considers a range of factors including the investee’s financial performance, progress against development milestones, funding status, observable transactions in comparable instruments, and broader market conditions.

The valuation of these investments involves estimation uncertainty, and actual outcomes may differ from those estimates. Changes in assumptions or the availability of market data could result in material adjustments to the carrying values of investments in future periods.
