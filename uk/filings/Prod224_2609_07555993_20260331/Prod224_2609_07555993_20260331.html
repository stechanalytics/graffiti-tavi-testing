The directors present their strategic report for the year ended 31 March 2026.

REVIEW OF BUSINESS
The directors are pleased with the performance of the business for the year ended 31 March 2026. Sales volumes were approximately 255 million litres, reflecting continued growth within the Company through a combination of organic expansion, new customer contracts and the continued integration of acquired businesses.

During the year the Company successfully completed the integration of the trade of Solo Petroleum Ltd, strengthening its presence across Yorkshire and further enhancing operational capability. The integration has delivered efficiencies across logistics, customer service and purchasing whilst providing a stronger platform for future growth.

The Group also completed the acquisition of the Dalston Fuel Terminal, representing a significant strategic investment in national infrastructure. The acquisition strengthens the Company's distribution network across the North of England and Scotland, improves operational efficiency through enhanced storage and logistics capabilities, and provides new opportunities for future expansion. The Directors believe the investment will improve resilience of supply, enhance customer service and support long-term sustainable growth.

The Company continues to prioritise long-term customer contracts over spot business, providing greater revenue stability and reducing exposure to market volatility within what remains a seasonal industry.
During the year the Company was also recognised in the Financial Times Europe's Long-Term Growth Champions 2026, reflecting the sustained growth achieved over the past decade, reflecting the dedication of its employees, the loyalty of its customers and the support of its business partners.

PRINCIPAL RISKS AND UNCERTAINTIES
Without mitigation the business could be exposed to a number of risks.

Credit Risk: The Company operates a strict credit control policy with the majority of our debt insured.

Environmental Risk: Standard Fuel Oils are exposed to environmental risks due to the nature of the products it delivers. The Company places significant emphasis on compliance ensuring that best practice is incorporated into its key processes. Our insurance policies remain effective enough to mitigate the risk of any unseen events.

Liquidity Risk: The Company continues to hold significant cash balances and has no long-term intention to move away from a policy of doing otherwise.

Competition: Fuel distribution is a highly competitive market, and although a significant amount of consolidation has taken place over the last decade, competition is still deemed to be the main force of regulation. We employ a dedicated and experienced sales team and have control over our own storage and logistics operation.

Major Disruption: The Company has a disaster recovery plan which is reviewed on an ongoing basis.

STREAMLINED ENERGY AND CARBON REPORTING
As part of our commitment to continuous improvement and environmental responsibility, our operations are aligned with three UKAS-certified ISO management systems, all of which are audited annually:

o ISO 9001 - Quality Management
o ISO 14001 - Environmental Management
o ISO 45001 - Occupational Health and Safety

These frameworks ensure that we set clear objectives and take effective action in areas of our business that have the greatest environmental impact.

Energy Use and Emissions
For the reporting period ending 31 March 2026, the Group's activities resulted in a total carbon footprint of 1,822 tonnes of CO2e, compared with 1,812 tonnes in the previous year. This represents a year-on-year increase from 10 tonnes in 2025 due to our increasing fleet size and acquisition of new sites. The intensity ratio is 6.63 tonnes of CO2e per million litres, compared to 7.65 in 2025.

During the year the Group's subsidiary company, Standard Fuel Oils Ltd, achieved certification as a Carbon Neutral Business through its partnership with Carbon Neutral Britain. This involved measuring the Company's greenhouse gas emissions in accordance with ISO 14064 and the GHG Emissions Protocol Accounting Standard before offsetting unavoidable emissions through independently verified carbon reduction projects.

Whilst offsetting forms part of our overall sustainability strategy, our primary focus remains the reduction of emissions at source. The acquisition by the Group of the Dalston Fuel Terminal is expected to reduce fleet mileage by enabling vehicles to operate closer to customers throughout Northern England and Scotland, improving both operational efficiency and environmental performance.

Electricity consumption during the year was 67,359 kWh (2025: 22,180 kWh), with the increase being driven by the new sites acquired in the current year. Energy usage continues to be closely monitored by the Directors with the objective of reducing consumption wherever practical.

Key Measures Implemented
To support the ongoing reduction of emissions and to manage resources more responsibly, the Company has taken the following actions across fleet, premises, and policy:

Carbon Reduction Initiatives
- Achieved Carbon Neutral Business certification through Carbon Neutral Britain.
- Completed a full greenhouse gas emissions assessment in accordance with ISO 14064 and the GHG
Emissions Protocol.
- Continued investment in initiatives to reduce emissions before offsetting unavoidable carbon emissions.
- Continued optimisation of delivery routes and logistics to minimise transport-related emissions.

Fleet Improvements
- Optimised the lifecycle management of company vehicles.
- Deployed new in-vehicle computer software to reduce paper consumption and enhance fuel efficiency.
- Continued promotion of a Green Car Scheme for employees.
- Continued promotion of the Cycle to Work Scheme.

Premises Enhancements
- Operated LED lighting across offices and operational premises.
- Added electric vehicle (EV) charging points to company sites.
- Utilised the upgraded heating system which uses a more environmentally friendly fuel.

Sustainable Company Policies
- Continued to raise workforce awareness of their role in maintaining our Carbon Neutral Business
certification.
- Operate flexible working practices to reduce travel-related emissions.
- Operate our vehicle fleet on Hydrotreated Vegetable Oil (HVO) - a fossil-free alternative to diesel, offering
up to 90% reduction in greenhouse gas emissions.
- Engaged with suppliers to encourage the setting and achievement of their own low-carbon targets.

FINANCIAL PERFORMANCE
Our turnover increased by 11% during the year, with sales volumes increasing to approximately 255 million litres. The Directors are satisfied with the results achieved, further strengthening the balance sheet with shareholders' funds of approximately £29.4m at the year end.

FUTURE DEVELOPMENTS
The Directors remain confident in the long-term prospects of the business whilst recognising the continuing uncertainty within energy markets.

Following the successful integration of Solo Petroleum Ltd and the acquisition by the Group of the Dalston Fuel Terminal, the company is well positioned to improve operational efficiency, strengthen its presence across Northern England and Scotland and continue expanding its customer base.

Taxation
Taxation for the year comprises current and deferred tax. Tax is recognised in the Statement of Comprehensive Income, except to the extent that it relates to items recognised in other comprehensive income or directly in equity.

Deferred tax
Deferred tax is recognised in respect of all timing differences that have originated but not reversed at the balance sheet date.

Timing differences arise from the inclusion of income and expenses in tax assessments in periods different from those in which they are recognised in financial statements. Deferred tax is measured using tax rates and laws that have been enacted or substantively enacted by the year end and that are expected to apply to the reversal of the timing difference.

Foreign currencies
