The director presents his strategic report for the year ended 30 April 2026.

REVIEW OF BUSINESS
iRob International Limited is an established automation engineering business providing robotic, tooling and turnkey automation solutions, primarily to customers operating within the automotive sector. Our experienced engineering team continues to deliver complex projects and bespoke manufacturing solutions, maintaining strong relationships with both existing and new customers whilst adapting to changing market conditions

Results and performance

The results for the year show a profit before taxation of £2,035,140 (2025: £13,474,955). Whilst turnover reduced to £20.8m from £48.1m in the previous year, reflecting the completion of a number of significant projects undertaken in the prior period, the company remained profitable and generated positive operating cash flows during the year. The Board considers the results to be satisfactory given the scale and timing of projects delivered during the period and remains focused on maintaining operational efficiency and sustainable profitability

Business environment

The automation and manufacturing sectors continue to experience varying levels of investment and project activity, creating both challenges and opportunities for the business. Despite a more subdued trading environment, the company has continued to strengthen relationships with key customers and pursue new opportunities across its target markets. The Board remains encouraged by the current pipeline of potential projects and believes the company's technical expertise, reputation and proven delivery capability position it well for future growth.

PRINCIPAL RISKS AND UNCERTAINTIES
The Board recognises that effective risk management is essential to the continued success of the business. Risk management is embedded throughout the organisation through a combination of policies, procedures and internal controls, all of which are subject to ongoing review by management and oversight by the Board. Compliance with legal, regulatory and ethical standards remains a key priority, whilst management continually monitors operational and financial risks to ensure the business remains well positioned to respond to changing market conditions

The principal risks from our business are as follows:

Market and customer concentration risk
The company's performance is influenced by levels of capital investment within the manufacturing and automotive sectors. Demand for automation solutions can fluctuate depending on customer investment cycles and wider economic conditions. The company mitigates this risk by maintaining strong relationships with existing customers whilst actively pursuing opportunities across a broader customer base and end markets.

Project delivery risk
The business undertakes complex engineering projects which require effective project management, resource planning and cost control. Failure to deliver projects on time and within budget could adversely affect profitability and customer relationships. Management continually monitors project performance and resource utilisation to minimise these risks

Competition and technological change
The automation sector remains highly competitive and is characterised by ongoing technological development. The company must continue to invest in its people, technical capabilities and innovation to ensure that it remains competitive and able to meet evolving customer requirements.

Liquidity and cash flow risk
The company regularly monitors its cash flow position and working capital requirements. Following the establishment of the Employee Ownership Trust during the year, future obligations associated with the transaction will be funded from future trading performance and cash generation. The Board therefore continues to place significant emphasis on cash flow forecasting, working capital management and maintaining appropriate liquidity levels

Credit risk
The company manages its exposure to customer credit risk through credit assessments, ongoing monitoring of customer balances and the use of contractual stage payments where appropriate. These procedures are designed to minimise exposure to potential bad debts and support effective cash collection

Economic uncertainty
The company's trading performance remains linked to broader economic conditions and levels of business investment. Management regularly reviews market activity, customer demand and future order opportunities to assess potential impacts on the business and to support informed strategic decision-making.

In light of these risks and uncertainties, the Board continues to monitor the company's operational and financial performance closely and believes it remains well positioned to pursue future opportunities whilst maintaining a strong and sustainable business model.

ENGAGEMENT WITH EMPLOYEES
The success of the company is built upon the skills, commitment and expertise of its employees. The Board recognises that maintaining an engaged, motivated and highly skilled workforce is fundamental to the delivery of high-quality engineering solutions and the continued success of the business. During the year, the company continued to invest in its employees through training, professional development and the provision of a safe and supportive working environment. Employee wellbeing, health and safety and personal development remain key priorities across all areas of the business.

The company is committed to maintaining open and effective communication with employees. Regular discussions are held between management and staff to provide updates on business performance, project activity and future opportunities, whilst also encouraging feedback and engagement at all levels of the organisation. The Board values the contribution made by employees and seeks to foster a culture of collaboration, accountability and continuous improvement.

During the year, the company completed its transition to employee ownership through an Employee Ownership Trust. The Board believes this structure further aligns the long-term interests of employees and the business, supporting a culture in which employees can share in the future success of the company and contribute to its continued development.

The company remains committed to providing equal opportunities for all employees and applicants for employment regardless of age, disability, gender reassignment, marriage and civil partnership status, pregnancy and maternity, race, religion or belief, sex or sexual orientation. Employment decisions are based on individual ability, qualifications and suitability for the role.

The Board would like to thank all employees for their continued hard work, flexibility and commitment throughout the year. Their dedication has enabled the company to remain profitable, deliver projects successfully and continue to support customers in a challenging market environment.

FUTURE DEVELOPMENTS
The Board remains focused on building long-term relationships with existing customers whilst developing opportunities with new customers and in adjacent market sectors. The company continues to see opportunities for automation and engineering solutions across a range of industries and will seek to leverage its technical expertise, strong reputation and proven project delivery capabilities to support future growth.

Investment in both employees and operational capabilities will remain a key priority to ensure the business is well positioned to meet customer requirements and respond to future market opportunities. The Board will continue to focus on operational efficiency, effective project delivery and the development of its skilled workforce to support sustainable long-term growth.

Following the transition to employee ownership during the year, the company is committed to generating sustainable profitability and strong cash flows for the benefit of all stakeholders. Whilst wider economic conditions remain uncertain, the directors believe the company is well placed to capitalise on future opportunities and maintain a strong financial position.

Significant judgements and estimates
In the application of the company's accounting policies, which are described in the accounting policies below, management is required to make judgements, estimates and assumptions about the carrying values of assets and liabilities that are not readily apparent from other sources. Revisions to accounting estimates are recognised in the period in which the estimate is revised if the revision affects only that period, or in the period of the revision and future periods if the revision affects both current and future periods.

Judgements
In preparing these financial statements, the directors have made the following key judgements that have a significant effect on the amounts recognised in the financial statements as described below.

- Determine whether there are indicators of impairment of the company's tangible assets along with residual values and asset lives. Estimates and assumptions
The key assumptions concerning the future and other key sources of estimation uncertainty at the reporting date, that have a significant risk of causing a material adjustment to the carrying amounts of assets and liabilities within the next financial year, are described below. Turnover
Turnover is measured at the fair value of the consideration received or receivable, excluding discounts, rebates, value added tax and other sales taxes.

Turnover represents the amounts (excluding value added tax) derived from the provision of goods and services to customers during the year.

Revenue is recognised when the significant risks and rewards of the goods or services provided have transferred to the buyer, the amount of revenue can be measured reliably and it is probable that the economic benefits associated with the transaction will flow to the company.

Revenue is measured at the fair value of the consideration receivable from the sale of goods and services to third parties. Revenue of the company comprises of the following key streams:

Sale of goods - Revenue on the sale of goods delivered is recognised when goods have been despatched to the customer. No revenue is recognised on work in progress.

Tangible fixed assets

Improvements to property - 10% on reducing balance

Tangible assets are stated at cost less accumulated depreciation and accumulated impairment losses. Cost includes the original purchase price, costs directly attributable to bringing the asset to its working condition for its intended use.

Stocks

Financial instruments

Current or deferred taxation assets and liabilities are not discounted.

Deferred tax
Deferred tax is recognised in respect of all timing differences that have originated but not reversed at the balance sheet date.

Timing differences arise from the inclusion of income and expenses in tax assessments in periods different from those in which they are recognised in financial statements. Deferred tax is measured using tax rates and laws that have been enacted or substantively enacted by the year end and that are expected to apply to the reversal of the timing difference.

Unrelieved tax losses and other deferred tax assets are recognised only to the extent that it is probable that they will be recovered against the reversal of deferred tax liabilities or other future taxable profits.

Research and development
Expenditure on research and development is written off in the year in which it is incurred.


Foreign currencies
In preparing the financial statements of the company, transactions in currencies other than the functional currency are recognised at the spot rate at the dates of the transactions, or at an average rate where this rate approximates the actual rate at the date of the transaction. At the end of each reporting period, monetary items denominated in foreign currencies are retranslated at the rates prevailing at that date. Non-monetary items that are measured in terms of historical cost in a foreign currency are not retranslated. Exchange differences are recognised in profit or loss in the period in which they arise or loss.

Pension costs and other post-retirement benefits
