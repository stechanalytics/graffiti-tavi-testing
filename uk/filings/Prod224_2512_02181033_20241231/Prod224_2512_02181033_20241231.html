STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 DECEMBER 2024


The Directors present their annual report and the financial statements of the Company for the year ended 31 December 2024.

REVIEW OF BUSINESS
Performance
The Company's total revenue in the year (2024) has decreased by 16.1% from the previous year (2023) with product sales being 19.1% down against 2022. The Company recorded an operating profit of £141k (£760k 2023), with a net profit of £100k (£532k 2023) for the financial year.

The organisation went through a significant restructure at the start of 2024, driven primarily by a large drop in sales. In Q1, the Company successfully implemented actions to mitigate for this loss of sales. The restructure, and a critical focus on business objectives, meant the end of year result was far better than that which had been planned. Other performance measures were not negatively impacted by the restructure, indicating that the organisation's agility and resilience were maintained despite job losses.

One key factor in the overall performance of the business was an asset impairment of circa £900k against equipment in the profit centre for shielding products. This had a direct impact on the end of year result. Even with this, the profit/loss was better than that planned.

A stable order book is visible up to 2029 and a business plan is in place showing a profitable situation for each of these years.

The Company was shielded somewhat by the fluctuation in energy costs by the fact that is has its own 500kW wind turbine. It was also able to negotiate reasonable terms for the energy supply it need in excess of that which it could produce itself.

The statement of financial position on page 9 of the financial statements shows the Company's financial position at the end of the year. The company has paid a dividend back to the shareholder in the sum of £1m

Measurement
The Company uses a number of financial and non-financial Key Performance Indicators ("KPIs") to measure performance, and these are reported at Board level and to senior management in regular meetings. The board considers that the Company has an effective measurement and reporting system, consistent with the Company's size and complexity. Performance against these KPIs during the year was as follows.

Key measurements of financial performance used by the Company are turnover, gross profit percentage and operating profit percentage calculated as reported on the face of the statement of comprehensive income. For the year under review, turnover was £19.24m (2023: £22.95m) with a gross profit of £1.884m (2023: £2.89m). The gross profit in percentage terms was 9.8% (2023: 12.6%), and the operating profit in percentage terms was 0.7% (2023: 3.3%).

Other key measures include; Return on Capital Employed at 0.1% (2023: 3.7%), Free Cash Flow of £3,257k (2023: £1,156k), and Net Working Capital ratio 25.5% (2023: 28.3%).

PRINCIPAL RISKS AND UNCERTAINTIES
Risk and uncertainties
Uncertainty is the main concern within the automotive business generally as the transformation from internal combustion engine (ICE) to electric driven vehicles (EV) is underway. This is a risk for the Company because its sales profile is almost exclusively focused on ICE engines. This uncertainty is not impacting on the Company immediately as we are seeing extensions of ICE engines from major OEMs. This means that continued sales are coming through for components that would have reached end of production had the transformation to EV been as rapid as expected. In the medium-term future, there will be an impact on the Company if new products for ICE vehicles are not brought into the plant.

The UK Government's ZEV mandate changed 7 April 2025. Before this date, it was defined that all new vehicles sold from 2030 must be purely EV drive. However, as a concession, the UK Government will allow the sale of hybrid cars up until 2035. This is a positive for the Company. However, the Company still needs to secure replacement work for ICE components. This strategy is being supported by a corporate (Group) strategy named Shape30, which is aimed at having 50% of sales related to EV drive systems by 2030.

The risk on customer dependency remains high. 2024 sales from the top three customers accounted for 68% of total parts sales. The loss of a major customer from the portfolio had a significant impact on this. To mitigate this risk, the Company continues to work towards developing revenue streams from alternative markets and customers.

Political upheaval in Europe with the war in Ukraine adds to the uncertainty of the situation in the automotive sector. From an economic point of view, the threat of tariffs on automotive vehicles, parts and raw materials will also leave a lot of uncertainty in the market. These tariffs, which if imposed, will ultimately impact on the whole automotive sector, affecting the Company.

FUTURE DEVELOPMENTS
The Company's primary market continues to be the automotive sector, developing relationships with an increasing number of new customers. The Company is also looking for potential in the market for small batch production and rapid prototyping, focusing on niche markets within the United Kingdom.

Taxation
Tax on the profit or loss for the year comprises current and deferred tax. Tax is recognised in the statement of comprehensive income except to the extent that it relates to items recognised directly in equity, in which case it is recognised in equity. Current tax is the expected tax payable on the taxable income for the year, using tax rates enacted or substantially enacted at the balance sheet date, and any adjustment to tax payable in respect of previous years.

Deferred tax is provided for using the balance sheet liability method, providing for temporary differences between the carrying amounts of assets and liabilities for financial reporting purposes and the amounts used for taxation purposes. Temporary differences are not provided for in relation to the initial recognition of assets or liabilities that affect neither accounting nor taxable profit. The amount of deferred tax provided is based on the expected manner of realisation or settlement of the carrying amount of assets and liabilities, using tax rates enacted or substantially enacted at the balance sheet date. A deferred tax asset is recognised only to the extent that it is probable that future taxable profits will be available against which the asset can be utilised. Deferred tax assets are reduced to the extent that it is no longer probable that the related tax benefit will be realised.

Deferred income tax assets and liabilities are offset when there is a legally enforceable right to offset current tax assets against current tax liabilities and when the deferred income taxes assets and liabilities relate to income taxes levied by the same taxation authority on either the taxable entity or different taxable entities where there is an intention to settle the balances on a net basis.

Research and development
Expenditure on development activities, involving a plan or design of new or substantially improved products or processes, is recognised in the statement of comprehensive income as an expense as incurred. R&D Tax credits are claimed and taxed as part of the Company's tax calculations. Assets pertaining to R&D projects have 100% tax allowances claimed.

Foreign currency
Transactions in foreign currencies are translated at the foreign exchange rate ruling at the date of the transaction. Monetary assets and liabilities denominated in foreign currencies at the balance sheet date are translated to pounds sterling at the foreign exchange rate ruling at that date. Foreign exchange differences arising on translation are recognised in the statement of comprehensive income in the period that they arise.
Non monetary items which are measured in terms of historical cost denominated in foreign currency are reported using the exchange rate at the date of the transaction, and non-monetary items which are carried at fair value denominated in a foreign currency are reported using the exchange rates that existed at the date when the values were determined.

Leases
With effect from 1 January 2019 leases are accounted for in accordance with IFRS 16 as adopted by the UK .The lease liability is initially recognised at the present value of the lease payments which have not yet been made and subsequently measured under the amortised cost method. The initial cost of the right-of-use asset comprises the amount of the initial measurement of the lease liability, lease payments made prior to the lease commencement date, initial direct costs and the estimated costs of removing or dismantling the underlying asset per the conditions of the contract.

Where ownership of the right-of-use asset transfers to the lessee at the end of the lease term, the right-of-use asset is depreciated over the assets remaining useful life. If ownership of the right-of-use asset does not transfer to the lessee at the end of the lease term, depreciation is charged over the shorter of the useful life of the right-of-use asset and the lease term.

Calculation of recoverable amount
The recoverable amount of assets is the greater of their net selling price and value in use. In assessing value in use, the estimated future cash flow are discounted to their present value using a pre-tax discount rate that reflects current market assessment of the time value of money and the risks specific to the asset. Liquidity risk is the risk of loss resulting from the inability to meet financial obligations in full and on time as they fall due. Liquidity needs are met through operating cash flows and the integration of a cash pooling arrangement within the Elring Klinger Group. This arrangement provides access to group-wide liquidity resources, enhancing the Company’s ability to meet its obligations.
