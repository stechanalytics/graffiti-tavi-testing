The Company operates within the core STEM specialist markets of digital, transformation and technology, focusing on the temporary and permanent supply of specialist staff to clients across a broad range of sectors, offering the most appropriate resourcing solutions for their requirements.

For the 52 weeks ended 2 January 2026, the Company generated turnover of £494.3m (2024: £487.6m, +1.4%), with activity remaining predominantly UK based (£490.5m, 99.2% of turnover). Gross profit for the period was £26.5m (2024: £27.6m, 4.1%), with the gross profit percentage reducing to 5.4% (2024: 5.7%). The reduction in gross profit reflects continued pressure within the recruitment market during 2025, including cautious client hiring behaviour and pricing pressure across contractor markets, partially offset by an increased contribution from permanent placements. Permanent fees represented 40.0% of gross profit (2024: 36.4%).

In response to the challenging operating environment, management took decisive action to align the cost base with trading conditions. Administrative expenses reduced to £22.3m (2024: £24.1m, 7.5%), driven by headcount reductions, property rationalisation and strict cost control measures. These actions supported improved operational efficiency and resulted in operating profit increasing to £4.2m (2024: £3.5m, +19.1%), with the operating profit conversion rate improving significantly to 15.7% (2024: 12.7%).

The balance sheet strengthened materially during the period, with net current liabilities of £11.7m compared to £67.5m in the prior year. This reflects changes in Group cash management arrangements following the refinancing of Group facilities, including the movement of intercompany balances, alongside strong operating cash generation in the second half of the year. Total shareholders’ funds reduced to £18.5m (2024: £35.4m), primarily reflecting dividends paid during the period rather than underlying trading performance.

The Directors recognise that the Company continues to operate within a macro economic environment characterised by inflationary pressures, elevated interest rates and geopolitical uncertainty, all of which impacted client confidence and hiring decisions during 2025. However, the business enters the new financial period with a more efficient operating model, improved cost discipline and resilient client relationships. While market conditions remain uncertain, the Directors believe the Company is well positioned to benefit from any sustained recovery in demand within its core digital and technology markets.

Principal risks and uncertainties

Attracting and retaining talent
Any constraints on the Company’s ability to attract and retain key talent in an increasingly competitive market could result in loss or weakening of client relationships, lack of appropriate leadership and/or erosion of the Company’s talent base, impacting achievement of both financial and other objectives.

Planned business transformation initiatives will create a need for new skill-sets in the Company in the medium term. Factors such as changes to the UK immigration rules may impact on the availability of talent more generally.

The Company’s high-retention business model ensures that brands and central functions are focused on talent management and development, performance review and succession planning. Leadership development programmes are in place and the Impellam Group’s Virtuoso-based approach encourages talent development and progression.

Customer concentration
General decline in a particular industry sector, loss of a key customer or a significant reduction in business volume on a key account could result in reduced revenue and/or increased pressure on gross profit. This exposure is known to have impacted on some of the Company’s competitors.

Management discuss and review market conditions and sales and account management pipelines on an ongoing basis. Management also hold regular meetings with key customers to discuss sales pipelines, current service performance and opportunities to add new services lines or extend existing services.

Technology Systems
The Company is reliant on many different technology systems that may have limited useful life in a fast-changing business environment. The legacy nature of some systems may hinder optimisation of end-to-end business processes. Systems may also be vulnerable to factors beyond the Company’s control e.g. power failures or internet connectivity outages.

The Company has a stable systems infrastructure and an ongoing IT investment programme. The Company is assessing the benefits of utilising systems that are now available within the wider group where they may enhance customer, colleague and worker experiences. In addition, the Company is assessing the use of AI to drive efficiencies and stakeholder benefits.

Cyber and Information security
The risk of external cyber-attacks continues to increase. A successful attack could result in loss of sensitive data, business disruption and/or damage to the Company’s reputation. A programme to enhance security of the Company’s systems against cyber-attack has been implemented and the business has been Cyber Essentials Plus certified since 2021.

Regulatory environment
Regulatory changes can lead to increased costs and workload, particularly where they relate to candidates’ rights, eligibility to work or corporate reporting e.g. payment practices, diversity.

Appropriate policies and codes of conduct are in place across the Company and regular training is provided to employees. External professional advice is sought where insufficient knowledge exists within the Company.

Going concern

As the financing of the Company is intrinsically tied to that of the wider HeadFirst Group, the going concern assessment has been done on a Group level rather than the Company level.

In assessing going concern, the Directors considered the financial position, liquidity and funding requirements of the Company and Group and its two separately financed sub-groups, Impellam Group Limited (Impellam) and HeadFirst Global B.V. (HeadFirst).

Impellam is forecast to remain within its available facilities and covenant requirements throughout the assessment period. However, HeadFirst is forecast to breach the covenants on its term loan facility in March 2027 and has limited covenant headroom prior to that date. In addition, the Group's non-convertible loan notes of £213.3m mature in March 2027 and the Group does not currently have sufficient committed facilities to meet these obligations.

To address these matters, management has commenced a structured Group-wide refinancing programme, supported by external financial advisers, and is actively progressing discussions with financing providers regarding the Company's and Group's future financing arrangements. However, refinancing agreements have not yet been concluded.

The Directors have concluded that it remains appropriate to prepare the Company's financial statements on a going concern basis. This conclusion reflects that the Company continues to trade profitably and generate operating cash flows; sufficient liquidity is forecast to be available during the assessment period provided refinancing initiatives are successfully implemented; the refinancing programme commenced well in advance of the relevant maturities and is supported by external advisers; discussions with financing providers are progressing; and management continues to implement operational initiatives to support profitability and liquidity improvements. However, the successful completion of the refinancing process is not wholly within the Company's control. The Directors nevertheless consider these actions to be achievable based on progress to date. Accordingly, these events and conditions indicate the existence of a material uncertainty that may cast significant doubt on the Company’s ability to continue as a going concern.

Impairment provisions for current and non-current trade debtors are recognised based on the simplified approach within IFRS 9 using a provision matrix in the determination of the lifetime expected credit losses. During this process the probability of the non-payment of the trade debtors is assessed. This probability is then multiplied by the amount of the expected loss arising from default to determine the lifetime expected credit loss for the trade debtors. Impairment provisions for receivables from related parties and loans to related parties are recognised based on a forward-looking expected credit loss model.
